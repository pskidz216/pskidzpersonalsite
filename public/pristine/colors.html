Pristine Coastal Co. color, August 2026

Replacing the green.

Intracoastal stays and Burnt Umber is out, so exactly one color is in play — the dark green anchor. Nothing on this page touches the ground, the coral or the haint blue. Six candidates, in two different ways of solving it.

What we are reacting to — #183F3B

PRISTINE.
COASTAL CO.

“The only thing I’m not sold on is that dark green — the other three colors are cool. It just doesn’t hit like Intracoastal for me.” The complaint is loudest toward the bottom of the page, where the anchor covers the most area.

There are two ways to fix it, and they are different bets. Move one keeps the structure exactly as it is and swaps the forest dark for a coastal dark. It is a one-token change: every page, sign and document reflows with a single find-and-replace.

Move two is the larger idea. The dark stops being the brand altogether: a light coastal color takes the big field, and the dark drops back to type only. That is what removes the heavy footer, which is the thing actually being reacted to — the objection is about volume as much as hue, and any dark, however coastal, still fills that space.

Every candidate clears 4.5:1 minimum for type, so none of this costs legibility on a yard sign or an estimate. The wordmark is unchanged throughout.

What is settled

The Intracoastal palette is confirmed and Burnt Umber is retired. Shell White, Atlantic Coral and Haint Blue are locked and are not part of this decision.

What one swap changes

The anchor is a single token across the concepts, the splash page, the signage and the paperwork. Whichever candidate wins, everything already built recolors from it — no rework.

What is needed

Arron’s own palette screenshots, and his pick for the replacement. His choice leads; these six are here so the conversation has something concrete to point at.
