The story

From the Corps to the coast.

The discipline came first. Before there was a company there was the Marine Corps, and the habits that stick with you after: you are early or you are late, the standard is the standard, and nobody gets to call a job finished except the person inspecting it.

Painting on the First Coast turned out to reward exactly those habits. Salt air punishes shortcuts here faster than almost anywhere in the country, and the difference between a four-year paint job and a ten-year one is almost entirely what happens before the color goes on. Twenty-two years later, that is still the whole business model: prep like it matters, put the price in writing, and film the finished work.

The recorded walkthrough started as a habit and became the signature. Every job ends with the crew walking every painted surface on camera, and the homeowner keeps the video. It settles questions before they become arguments, and it means our standard is checkable, not claimed.

The process

Every job runs the same five steps.

01

The walkthrough

We walk the job with you, on camera. What gets painted, what gets repaired, what it costs. You keep the video, so nothing said at the estimate can drift later.

02

The price, in writing

A written estimate with the prep listed line by line. The price is the price; it moves only if the scope does, and you approve that in writing too.

03

Prep like it matters

Wash, scrape, repair, sand, prime. Most of the job happens here, which is why our exteriors last eight to ten years on a coast that eats paint in four.

04

Paint, contained

Rooms contained, floors covered, landscaping protected, and the site cleaned and reset every evening. You keep living your life while we work.

05

The recorded walkthrough

The job ends the way it started: every surface on camera, with you holding the video. Done means documented.

What we hold ourselves to

Three rules, no exceptions.

Show up when we said

Early or late; there is no third option. The schedule is in writing and weather is the only thing allowed to move it, with a phone call, not a silence.

The price is the price

Published ranges, a written estimate, and no number that grows in the dark. If we misjudged the work, that is our tuition, not your bill.

Leave it cleaner

Every evening the site is reset: floors clear, drops folded, walkways open. The last day looks like we were never there, except for the paint.
