FAQ

Twenty questions, answered straight.

Everything people ask before hiring a painter on the First Coast, answered the way we answer them on the phone. If yours is not here, call — the estimate is free and so is the conversation.

Cost & the estimate

How much does painting cost in Jacksonville?−

Interiors typically run $2.45 to $5.50 per square foot and exteriors $3.05 to $6.90, driven by prep, substrate and finish level. The full table and a ten-second calculator are on our pricing page, published, not gated.

Is the estimate really free?+

Yes. We walk the job, record the walkthrough, and put the price in writing. No deposit to get a number, and the price does not move unless the scope does.

Do you require a deposit?+

Never to get an estimate. If a job requires any payment schedule, it is written into the estimate before you sign, so nothing about money is a surprise.

What is included in the estimate?+

Prep, surface repairs, materials, the number of coats and cleanup, with assumptions spelled out. Anything excluded is excluded in writing, not discovered later.

Why do you publish your prices?+

Because gating a range loses the people who came to find it, and because a company confident in its numbers has no reason to hide them. The walkthrough turns the range into your exact quote.

The work itself

How much prep do you actually do?+

Prep is most of the job: wash, scrape, repair, sand, prime. It is listed line by line in the estimate so you can see exactly what you are paying for before a drop of color goes on.

What paint do you use?+

The right system for the surface and the exposure, named in the estimate. Coastal exteriors get marine-grade coatings; interiors get washable finishes matched to the room. We do not swap products after the quote.

Can you match my existing wall texture?+

Yes. Knockdown, orange peel, skip trowel or smooth — the patch gets textured to disappear before it gets painted, and we sample it dry before committing.

Is two coats really two coats?+

Coverage is specified, not eyeballed. If a color needs a third coat to hold, it gets one, and the estimate already said so.

How do you handle old lead paint?+

Homes built before 1978 get lead-safe prep: containment, no open dry sanding, and a full cleanup at the end of every day.

Scheduling & living through it

How long will my job take?+

Most interiors run two to five days and most single-family exteriors one to two weeks. The schedule is in the written estimate, and weather moves it with a phone call, not a silence.

Can you work while we are living in the house?+

Yes, most of our interior work is in occupied homes. Rooms are contained, cleaned and reset every day, and the schedule is agreed before we start.

Do I need to be home while you work?+

No. Many clients hand us a key or a code. Every stage is photographed, decisions can be approved by phone, and the recorded walkthrough lets you inspect whenever suits you.

What happens if it rains?+

Exterior work pauses and you get a call the same day with the new schedule. Interiors continue. Weather is the one thing allowed to move a date.

What does cleanup actually look like?+

Daily, not end-of-job. Floors clear, drops folded, walkways open every evening. The last day looks like we were never there, except for the paint.

Situations

Do you handle storm and water damage?+

Yes. Restoration is half our work: drywall, trim and repaint after a leak or a storm, documented so your insurance has what it needs.

Do you work with HOA and historic district approvals?+

Yes. We prepare the color and sheen submission with you and confirm what needs sign-off before any work is scheduled, so nothing gets painted twice.

Can you work around a rental schedule?+

Yes. We schedule between turnovers, contain and clean daily, and photograph before and after so you can see every unit without driving out.

Can you match the existing color after a repair?+

Yes. We pull the match from a protected surface rather than a faded one, and we spray out a sample on the wall before committing.

What is the recorded walkthrough?+

Every job starts and ends with a walk of every painted surface on camera, and you keep both videos. The first locks the scope; the second proves the finish.

Still have a question?

Ask it on the walkthrough. It is free, it is recorded, and it usually takes less than an hour.
