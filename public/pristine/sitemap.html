Why not one single page

Search is won page by page. A San Marco page wins “painter near San Marco”, a section anchor does not, and one URL cannot rank for fourteen different local searches. AI answer engines quote pages, not scroll positions. A single page would also carry every image on one load, and the current site’s 1.37 second load is worth protecting.

What gets retired

The fourteen junk location pages, state parks and towns two hours away, plus roughly twenty-two spun blog posts. Every one redirects to its closest new home, so nothing already earned in search is thrown away. Thirty-one thin pages become fourteen real ones.

Two naming changes

/service-areas becomes /neighborhoods, the word customers actually use. /cost becomes /pricing: cost reads cold, investment reads evasive, and estimate collides with the button sitting beside it.
